Pre-Mobility Activities

Section 1: why develop global competence, how to evaluate it, and what I want to achieve.

Why develop your Global Competence?

Activity 1

I agree that developing Global Competence in young people is a good idea. Meeting people from a different nationality, ethnicity, religion or culture pushes you to open up to the world, and I think that shapes the way you end up thinking later on, not just during the exchange itself.

Since I've been in Taiwan I've noticed this mostly through language. At the university it's fairly easy to get by in English, but outside, in the street or in most shops, almost nobody speaks it, which is very different from France where even older people usually know a few basic words. It's made me realise how much I took that for granted.

This also connects to what I want to do later: become an engineer in innovation, maybe in aeronautics or automotive, though nothing is set yet. I always pictured myself staying in France, because I really like the country, but being here for a few months has made me question that a bit more than before.

I'm less convinced that this kind of mobility makes you an engaged actor on global issues like poverty or climate change. Four months feels short for that. I think it's a first step towards being aware of these problems rather than actual training to deal with them. On media bias I'd say I'm reasonably careful, even if I spend a lot of time on screens, but I can still fall for something without noticing.

Evaluating your Global Competence.

Activity 2: the Global Mind Monitor (GMM)

What does the GMM help you to do?

It helps you learn more about your global competence, whether for personal development, education or your professional path. You can access your questionnaires, track your results and progress through statistics, compare your scores, and complete self-assessments on things like open-mindedness or cultural awareness.

Who developed the GMM?

It was presented by Prof. Dr. Ankie Hoefnagels from Zuyd University of Applied Sciences and the Research Centre Global Minds at Work. It's designed for teachers and educators, and for students or professionals who want to develop and measure their intercultural skills.

Thinking ahead.

Activity 4

How will your mobility help you develop your competence over the next four months?

I think what will help me progress the most is the daily conversations I'll have with locals and all the interactions that come from meeting new people at Ming Chi University of Technology, whether it's during classes, sport activities, or just dealing with admin stuff. I'm also planning to make the most of the French Week (which hasn't happened yet) to get more familiar with the culture and push myself further.

What objectives do you want to achieve in that short time?

The one that matters most to me is going back to France fully bilingual. I would also like to become more comfortable with the people I've just met here, especially those I have nothing in common with: not the culture, not the language, not even the way they see the world. Beyond that, I really want to travel and see as much as possible while I'm here, so I don't come back with any regrets.
